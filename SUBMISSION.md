## Title

The Monsters Changed. The Fear Didn't. — Halloween DataViz Contest 2026

## Post body

The insight. As children, we feared what we could see. As adults, we fear what we can’t.

105 people told the Fabric community what scared them as kids and what scares them now. 43% still carry at least one childhood fear; 57% left them all behind. But the replacements are a different kind of monster. “Dark, unknown & supernatural” fears fall from 46% of people to 13%, and every other concrete category falls too. “Performance & future” rises from 10% to 40%, “Separation & loss” from 18% to 38%. War is now the most common adult fear, named by 19 people, up from 5. And some monsters never leave: 6 of the 11 people who feared snakes as children still do.

How it was built. One A4 portrait page, Power BI only, on the organizer’s starter model. The bedroom-to-office illustration is a custom SVG set as the page background, with seven small looping GIFs in native image visuals that bring it to life: the ghost drifts, the lamp and the monster’s eyes flicker, office windows switch on and off, and a light travels from the bedroom to the office. Every number is a live native card or chart; nothing is typed in. Added measures cover people keeping or dropping a childhood fear, selections per fear and stage, “still afraid” (same person, same fear, both stages) and share of people per category.

Honesty notes. Each person named up to three fears per stage, so category shares are “share of people naming at least one fear in that category”. Categories are the survey author’s. Respondents are self-selected and skew male and aged 25 to 44. Source values unchanged, no outside data.

Accessibility. Orange for childhood, blue for adulthood, both above 3:1 on the dark background and distinguishable under colour-vision deficiency. Every bar and card carries a visible value. Text sits on dark panels at 4.5:1 or better. Tab order follows the story with the decorative animations excluded, and every data visual has alt text that states its insight.
